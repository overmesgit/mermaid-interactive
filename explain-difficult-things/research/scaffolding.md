# Scaffolding

Temporary support so a learner can do something they cannot yet do alone, removed as they take it over. Included because the other notes are about content order and representation. This one is about how much help to give in the moment.

## Wood, Bruner, and Ross (1976)

The paper usually cited for the teaching metaphor. The Educational Psychology Review survey (van de Pol, Volman, & Beishuizen, 2010) lists their six scaffolding functions as:

1. Recruitment (getting the learner engaged in the task)
2. Reduction of degrees of freedom (simplifying the task)
3. Direction maintenance (keeping the learner on the goal)
4. Marking critical features (pointing at what matters)
5. Frustration control
6. Demonstration

The same review says the idea is older than that paper. Ausubel (1963) used "ideational scaffolding" as a metaphor for a temporary support of ideas, without defining a teaching method. See [ausubel-meaningful-learning.md](ausubel-meaningful-learning.md).

A historical article (Shvarts & Bakker, 2019) argues against the claim that Wood, Bruner, and Ross invented the metaphor, and traces related uses through Vygotsky's circle. Treat "Vygotsky invented scaffolding" as loose. What is Vygotsky's is the **zone of proximal development**: the distance between what the learner can do alone and what they can do with a more capable other. Wood et al. describe the practical effect as task competence developing faster than unassisted effort (1976, p. 90, as cited in that historical article).

## A later teaching framework

van de Pol, Volman, and Beishuizen separate **intentions** (what is being supported: cognitive, metacognitive, affective) from **means** (how): feeding back, hints, instructing, explaining, modeling, and questioning. They also use Tharp and Gallimore's (1988) six means of assisting performance: modeling, contingency management, feeding back, instructing, questioning, and cognitive structuring.

The review's Vygotskian point, via Stone (1993): both people in the exchange are active. Support is contingent on the learner's current attempt, not a fixed script.
