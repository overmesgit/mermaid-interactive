# Make them do something

Ask the reader to predict or explain something, not "does that make sense?"

## Why

Reading an explanation feels like understanding, and the feeling is unreliable. People rate their understanding much lower after trying to explain a mechanism step by step. Learning grows with what the reader does: reading is weakest, highlighting or clicking is a little better, producing something new (a prediction, an explanation, a comparison) is much better, and a real back-and-forth where both sides add ideas is best. "Makes sense?" invites a yes, which shows nothing.

## How

- Before a step, ask the reader to predict what happens next.
- After an example, ask which part was the idea.
- Ask for a short causal account: "In two sentences, why does an order stop at Placed?"
- Ask them to compare two cases and name the difference.
- In a chat, keep turns short enough that the reader answers, not just reads.

## Example

After showing the login sequence, ask: "If the password is wrong, what does the web app get back from the API?" The answer (no cookie, so the reader is not logged in) shows they followed where the session comes from.

## Watch out

- A reader who repeats the right words may still not understand. Ask for a new case, not a definition.
- A reader who is already overloaded gains nothing from a prompt. Reduce the material first.

Based on: Chi and Wylie's ICAP framework; self-explanation (Chi); the illusion of explanatory depth (Rozenblit and Keil); mimicry (Meyer and Land).
