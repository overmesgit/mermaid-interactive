# Adjust the help

Give less help when the reader succeeds and more when they stall.

## Why

Help is a temporary support. The right amount sits just beyond what the reader can do alone. Too little, and they stall. Too much, and it gets in the way: an expert slowed by step-by-step detail learns less, not more. The level of help is not fixed. It should follow what the reader just showed they can do.

## How

- Start with the level that fits the reader (see [01-know-the-reader.md](01-know-the-reader.md)).
- If they answer a check correctly, drop a level: fewer steps shown, more left to them.
- If they stall, add a level: a hint, then a narrower question, then a worked step.
- Keep them pointed at the goal. Mark what matters now, and hold off side topics.
- Remove the support as they take over: full example, then partial, then their own.

## Example

A senior developer reviewing a PR needs the one-paragraph "why" and the diff of the state diagram. A new team member on the same PR needs the small whole first, then the steps. Offer the short version and let them open more (drill-down, panel notes).

## Watch out

- A fixed, fully worked explanation for everyone is the most common mismatch.
- Removing help too early feels like being left alone. Fade it step by step.

Based on: scaffolding (Wood, Bruner, and Ross; van de Pol and colleagues); fading worked examples (Renkl); the expertise reversal effect (Kalyuga).
