# DeFT (Shaaron Ainsworth)

A framework for learning with more than one external representation. DeFT is Design, Functions, Tasks. Multiple representations sometimes help and often do not. The framework is a way to ask why a second diagram, table, or equation is there, and what the learner has to do with it.

The 2006 paper is the conceptual statement. The 2001 paper with Van Labeke, which was fetched, summarizes the same three functions and five design parameters and applies them to instructional simulations.

## Functions

Three jobs a second representation can do:

- **Complement.** Different processes, or different information, so each form carries something the other does not.
- **Constrain.** One representation limits how the other can be misread. A familiar form can make an unfamiliar form interpretable. The 2001 paper's example type is a concrete picture beside a graph.
- **Construct.** The learner abstracts what is invariant across the representations and what belongs only to one of them. That is the route to a deeper account and, in Ainsworth's argument, to transfer.

If a second representation does none of these, it is cost: the learner still has to learn the format and translate between formats.

## Tasks the learner must do

Understand the properties of each representation and how it relates to the domain. Sometimes select, construct, or invent a representation. The task that exists only because there are two representations is translation between them. The 2001 paper says there is abundant evidence that translation is difficult.

## Design parameters (Van Labeke & Ainsworth, 2001)

1. **Redundancy.** How information is spread across the representations, and how much is repeated.
2. **Form.** The computational properties of the system, for example text with graphics.
3. **Translation support.** None, highlighting of correspondences, or full linking so a change in one representation appears in the other.
4. **Sequence.** Order, and when the set on screen changes, if not all are shown at once.
5. **Number.** How many representations.

## A note on data

DeFT is not a chart-choosing catalog. It is the test for any encoding, including a table of values, a chart of a pattern, or a diagram of a mechanism: what does this form compute that the words do not, does it block a misreading, or is the learner supposed to see the invariant? Mayer's contiguity and redundancy principles (see [mayer-multimedia.md](mayer-multimedia.md)) are about how to place and how not to duplicate. DeFT is about whether the extra form should exist.
