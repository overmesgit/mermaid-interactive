# Curse of knowledge and expert blind spot (Hinds; Nathan, Koedinger, and Alibali)

The explainer's side of the problem. Every other note is about the learner. This one is about why the person who understands the subject is often bad at judging what is hard about it.

This note is thinner than the others. Neither primary paper was opened. Treat it as a lead.

## Curse of expertise (Hinds, 1999)

From search summaries of the paper: people with different levels of experience predicted how long novices would take to learn to use a mobile phone. Experts underestimated novice time more than people with less experience did. The debiasing methods tested (in the title of the paper) are reported as not removing the bias, including asking experts to remember their own learning. Exact figures were not checked.

## Expert blind spot (Nathan, Koedinger, and Alibali, 2001)

A conference paper. Koedinger's own description of the term: experiments showing that domain experts "can be systematically inaccurate in judging the difficulties of novice users/students." Nathan and Petrosino (2003) extended the term to preservice teachers. Open that paper before saying what it found.

## Same point, from sources already in this folder

- Land et al. (2005, p. 58): it is difficult for teachers "who long since travelled similar ground in their own disciplinary excursions, to gaze backwards across thresholds." See [threshold-concepts.md](threshold-concepts.md).
- Taber's pedagogic impediment: the teacher's own simplification or ordering creates the wrong idea, and neither side notices until the test. See [taber-prior-knowledge.md](taber-prior-knowledge.md).
- Perkins's tacit knowledge: what the expert no longer says because it no longer feels like a step.

## Why it matters for a model writing explanations

A language model explaining code has read more of the subject than almost any reader, and has no memory of having learned it. That is the curse in its strongest form. Hinds's result suggests that telling the explainer to "remember being a beginner" is not a fix. The fixes the other notes offer are external: ask what the person already thinks (Ausubel, Taber), ask them to predict or explain (ICAP), name the step that is usually skipped (Catrambone's subgoal labels in [examples.md](examples.md)).
